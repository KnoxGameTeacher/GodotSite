Section 10: Projectiles & Damage

Fire projectiles that deal damage to enemies.

Section Progress: 0%
1

Repeatable Projectiles

  • Create a new scene with an Area3D root
  • Rename it to "Projectile"
  • Add a MeshInstance3D child and give it a shape for what your tower shoots. This depends on what your projectile is.
  • Add a CollisionShape3D child and give it a shape that covers the mesh.
  • Save the scene as scenes/projectile.tscn
  • Attach a script and save it as scripts/projectile.gd
💡 GDD Check: Check your GDD. What does your tower shoot? That answer is your projectile model. It could be a simple primitive sphere, or it could be a plant pot.

Instantiation and Deletion

  • Add this to scripts/projectile.gd:

extends Area3D

@export var speed = 20.0
@export var lifetime = 3.0

var direction: Vector3
var damage = 0

func _process(delta):
	global_position += direction * speed * delta
	lifetime -= delta
	if lifetime <= 0:
		queue_free()
  • Mine is 20 for speed, but yours can be anything. Your GDD decides how fast your shots fly.
  • Direction and damage are set by the tower when it fires. The projectile itself does not decide them.
  • The flight is straight. A fast projectile at a slow enemy usually hits, fast enemies might escape.
  • The lifetime countdown is cleanup. A projectile that misses flies on, then frees itself instead of piling up forever.

Attacking Spawns a Shot

  • Open scripts/tower.gd and add an export for the projectile scene:
@export var projectile_scene: PackedScene
  • Drag projectile.tscn into the projectile_scene slot in the Inspector
  • Replace the direct damage line in the ATTACKING branch with a shot. The branch now reads:
		
State.ATTACKING:
    if not is_instance_valid(target):
        target = null
        state = State.IDLE
    else:
        fire_projectile()
        cooldown_timer.start()
        state = State.COOLDOWN
  • Add fire_projectile to scripts/tower.gd:

func fire_projectile():
	var projectile = projectile_scene.instantiate()
	get_parent().add_child(projectile)
	projectile.global_position = global_position
	projectile.direction = (target.global_position - global_position).normalized()
	projectile.damage = attack_damage
  • add_child comes before setting global_position, the same order as Section 7 taught you.
  • The direction is set once, at fire time, from the tower to where the target stands right now.
  • The damage comes from the tower's attack_damage.
💡 Note: Your projectile starts at the tower's origin. If that looks wrong for your model, raise the spawn point above the origin.

Handle Collisions

  • Connect the projectile Area3D body_entered signal to the projectile script
  • Add the handler to scripts/projectile.gd:

func _on_body_entered(body):
	if body is Enemy:
		body.take_damage(damage)
		queue_free()
  • body_entered fires when the projectile touches a physics body. Enemies are CharacterBody3D, so they count. Zones are Area3D, so they do not.
  • The is check matters. Without it, anything the projectile touches would get take_damage called on it, and most things do not have that function.
  • take_damage is exactly the function from Section 9, nothing about damage changed, only the delivery.
  • The projectile frees itself on a hit, so one shot damages one enemy.
💡 Note: Projectiles work on any enemy, so waves in Section 12 need no projectile changes.

Testing Shots and Hits

  • Place a tower near the path, the way Section 7 taught you.
  • Press F6. An enemy walks the path from Section 5.
  • When the enemy comes in range, a projectile appears at the tower and flies toward it.
  • On a hit, the projectile disappears and the enemy loses health. With a temporary print in take_damage, you can watch it drop.
  • After enough hits, the enemy dies mid-path instead of reaching the Base.
  • Fire at an enemy that leaves range. The projectile flies on, then disappears when its lifetime runs out. Nothing piles up.
  • Place a second tower. Projectiles from both towers hit.
💡 If the projectile will not fire:
  • Projectile never appears: the projectile_scene export slot is empty, or fire_projectile is not called. Check the ATTACKING branch.
  • Projectile hangs in the air: direction was never set, or speed is 0. Check fire_projectile and the speed export.
  • Projectile passes through enemies: the body_entered signal is not connected, or the CollisionShape3D is missing or too small.
  • Projectile hits but the enemy takes no damage: damage was never set. Check the projectile.damage = attack_damage line.
  • Error about take_damage on something that is not an enemy: the is Enemy check is missing from the handler.
  • Projectiles pile up forever: the lifetime countdown is missing from _process.
  • Enemy dies on the first hit: check your GDD numbers. attack_damage may be higher than max_health.
