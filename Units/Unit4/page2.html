Section 2: Project Setup & Environment

Create the Blocky Blocks project and build the environment where your tower defense game will take place.

Section Progress: 0%
1

Setting Up the Project

  • Open Godot and click "New Project"
  • Name your project. Mine is "Blocky Blocks", but yours can be anything you like
  • Choose a location on your computer
  • Select "Compatibility" as the renderer
  • Click "Create & Edit"
2

Organizing Your Project

A clean folder structure saves you time as your project grows. Create these folders in your FileSystem dock:

  • scenes/ — for all your .tscn scene files
  • scripts/ — for standalone .gd scripts
  • assets/ — for imported models, textures, and sounds
  • resources/ — for custom resources like wave definitions
Inside assets/
  • towers/ — your custom tower models from Asset Forge
  • enemies/ — enemy models
  • environment/ — path, terrain, and scenery models
  • sounds/ — music and sound effects
💡 Your GDD Guides This: If your GDD calls for features like tower upgrades or multiple enemy types, you may want additional subfolders. Organize for the game you're actually building, not just the structure I'm using.
3

Getting Environment Models

You'll need 3D models for your game environment. Here is a limited list of options:

  • Itch.io Free 3D Assets — Browse free 3D assets
  • Kay Lousberg — kaylousberg.com/game-assets
  • Kenney — kenney.nl/assets
  • Craftpix — https://craftpix.net/categorys/3d-game-assets/

You'll need:

  • Ground or terrain of some sort
  • Scenery (rocks, trees, buildings, or decorative items)
  • A "base" or "goal" object that enemies are trying to reach
  • A "spawn point" marker for where enemies appear
💡 Your GDD Guides This: What setting did you choose for your tower defense game? Forest? Castle? Space station? Pick assets that match your GDD's visual direction.

4

Creating the Environment Scene

  • Create a new scene with a Node3D as the root
  • Rename it to "Environment"
  • Save it as "environment.tscn" inside your scenes/ folder
Adding the Ground
  • Add a MeshInstance3D as a child
  • Rename it to "Ground"
  • Assign a ground mesh — you can use a PlaneMesh or an imported terrain model
  • Add a StaticBody3D as a child of Ground
  • Add a CollisionShape3D to the StaticBody3D
  • Set its shape to match the ground (typically a BoxShape3D or WorldBoundaryShape3D)
💡 Structural Note: The exact size and shape of your ground depend on how big your path and placement zones will be. Design the ground to comfortably fit your play area with a little extra room around the edges.
Figure 2.4: Setting up the ground with collision
5

Base (the Player's Goal to Defend)

  • Add a Node3D as a child of Environment
  • Rename it to "Base"
  • Add a MeshInstance3D as a child of Base
  • Assign a model or primitive mesh that clearly represents a "goal"
  • Position the Base at one end of your planned path

Spawn Point (Where Enemies Appear)

  • Add a Node3D as a child of Environment
  • Rename it to "SpawnPoint"
  • Position it at the opposite end of the path from the Base
  • You don't need a visual model here — this is a marker that the Wave Manager will use later
💡 Your GDD Guides This: Look at your Path Design section. Is your path straight, curved, or branching? The Base and Spawn Point should be at the two ends of your main path.
Figure 2.5: Adding the Base and Spawn Point markers
6

Decorating Your Environment

  • Place decorative models (rocks, trees, buildings) around the play area
  • Keep the path itself clear so enemies can walk freely
  • Scenery can also visually indicate where players can and can't place towers

Lighting the Scene

  • Add a DirectionalLight3D as a child of Environment
  • Rotate it to create the lighting direction you want
  • Add a WorldEnvironment node for sky and ambient light
  • In the Inspector, click "New Environment"
  • Set the background to a Sky and choose an atmosphere that matches your game's mood
💡 Your GDD Guides This: Your game's mood and setting come from your GDD. A bright daytime castle feels very different from a moody nighttime fortress. Choose lighting and scenery that support the experience you're designing.
