Section 4: Creating the Enemy Scene

Build your first enemy scene. Every enemy type gets its own scene, and all of them share one script.

Section Progress: 0%
1

Building the Enemy Scene

  • Create a new scene with a CharacterBody3D as the root
  • Rename it to "Enemy" + the enemy type (e.g., "Enemy_Goblin")
  • Drag your enemy model file from the FileSystem dock into the Scene dock to instance it as a child
  • Rename the model child to "Model"
  • Add a CollisionShape3D as a child and give it a shape that fits the model
  • Save it as "enemy.tscn" inside your scenes/ folder
  • This scene is your basic enemy type, not a generic template. Later enemy types get their own scene files.
Why CharacterBody3D
  • It is built for things that move and collide
  • It stays on the ground and slides along obstacles instead of getting stuck
💡 GDD Check: What does your enemy look like? Check your GDD. A player should tell it apart from a tower at a glance, just like your towers are distinct from each other.
Figure 4.1: The enemy scene structure

Stats in the Inspector

  • Attach a new script to the Enemy root and save it as scripts/enemy.gd
  • Add your stats as @export variables, like this:
extends CharacterBody3D

@export var max_health = 100
@export var move_speed = 3.0
@export var damage = 1
@export var gold_reward = 10
  • @export puts these in the Inspector, so you can tune them without opening the script
  • Keep stats as variables, not constants. Later sections will change them while the game runs, and constants cannot be modified at runtime.
  • This one script will be shared by every enemy scene you make.
💡 GDD Check: Pull your starting values from your GDD. For example, if your basic enemy should die to two tower hits, set max_health to twice your tower damage.
Figure 4.2: Enemy stats as export variables in the Inspector
3

Tagging with Groups

  • Select the Enemy root and open the Node dock
  • Go to the Groups tab and add the enemy to a group called "enemies"
  • In Section 8, your towers will search this group to find targets
  • Every enemy scene you make later gets the same group tag
Figure 4.3: Adding the enemy to the enemies group
4

Testing the Scene

  • Open environment.tscn
  • Drag enemy.tscn into the scene, near your SpawnPoint
  • Press F6. The enemy should rest on the ground, not fall through it.
  • It will not move yet. Movement comes in Section 5.
💡 Errors you might encounter:
  • Check the red output text. The error names the script and the line number. Open that script, go to that line, and start there.
  • Enemy falls through the ground: the CollisionShape3D is missing or has no shape. Recheck step 1.
  • Export variables missing from the Inspector: the script is not saved or not attached to the Enemy root.
  • Model file will not drop onto a MeshInstance3D: .glb files are scenes, not meshes. Drag the file into the Scene dock to instance it instead.
  • To check if a script runs at all, add print("test") to it and look for the message in the Output panel at the bottom of the editor.
Figure 4.4: A test enemy resting in the environment
5

One Script, One Scene Per Type

  • Pick a second enemy type from your GDD
  • In the FileSystem dock, duplicate your enemy scene and rename the copy after your new type
  • Open the copy, delete the Model child, and drag in your new enemy type's model file
  • Rename the new model child to "Model" and the root to "Enemy_[Type]"
  • Resize the CollisionShape3D to fit the new model
  • Tune the @export stats in the Inspector for the new type
  • Check the copy still uses scripts/enemy.gd. Duplicate the scene, never the script.
  • The "enemies" group tag copies with the scene, so the new enemy is already tagged
  • Drag the copy into environment.tscn and press F6 to check it rests on the ground like the first one
💡 GDD Check: Your second enemy should play a different role from your basic enemy. A fast enemy and a tank enemy need different stats, not just different models.
