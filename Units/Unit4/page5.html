Section 5: Path Following with Path3D

Give your enemies a path to march along.

Section Progress: 0%
1

Drawing the Path

  • Open environment.tscn
  • Add a Path3D as a child of Environment
  • Rename it to "Path"
  • In the Inspector, set Curve to New Curve3D
  • Select Path, then use the Add Point tool in the toolbar and click in the viewport to place points along your planned route
  • Place your first point at the SpawnPoint and your last point at the Base
💡 GDD Check: Check your GDD path design. Your path should run from SpawnPoint to Base.
Figure 5.1: Path3D with curve points
2

Carrying the Enemy

  • Add a PathFollow3D as a child of Path
  • Rename it to "PathFollow"
  • In the Inspector, turn off Loop. With Loop on, the enemy wraps back to the start instead of stopping at the Base.
  • Drag your basic test enemy onto PathFollow in the Scene dock. Leave your second enemy in the environment for now. It gets its own PathFollow in step 5.
  • In the Inspector, set the enemy Position to (0, 0, 0) so it sits exactly on the path
Figure 5.2: PathFollow3D in the scene tree
3

Following the Path in Code

  • Open scripts/enemy.gd
  • Add a _process function that moves the parent PathFollow3D:
func _process(delta):
	var follow = get_parent() as PathFollow3D
	follow.progress += move_speed * delta
	if follow.progress_ratio >= 1.0:
		# Enemy reached the end of the path
		follow.queue_free()
  • progress is the distance along the path, so adding move_speed times delta moves the enemy at move_speed units per second
  • progress_ratio runs from 0.0 at the start to 1.0 at the end. When it reaches 1.0, the enemy is at the Base.
  • queue_free on the follow removes the follow and the enemy with it
  • Use _process because the path moves the enemy, not physics
  • For now the enemy just despawns at the end. Base damage comes later.
Figure 5.3: Enemy movement code in enemy.gd
4

Testing the Walk

  • Press F6. The enemy should walk along the path from SpawnPoint toward Base.
  • When it reaches the end, it despawns.
💡 If your enemy won't walk:
  • Enemy does not move, or you get an error about progress: the enemy must be a child of a PathFollow3D. Check the Scene dock.
  • Enemy wraps back to the start instead of stopping: Loop is still on. Turn off Loop on the PathFollow3D.
  • Enemy floats or sinks: the path points are not at ground height. Move the points to sit on the ground.
  • Enemy walks backward: your model's front faces the wrong way. Rotate the Model child 180 degrees around Y.
  • Only one enemy type moves, or enemies behave differently: you may have duplicated enemy.gd instead of the scene. Check that every enemy scene uses the same scripts/enemy.gd file.
  • Enemy walks past the end without despawning: the check must be >= 1.0, not == 1.0, because progress can overshoot the end between frames.
Figure 5.4: Enemy walking the path
5

Two Enemies, Two Speeds

  • Add a second PathFollow3D as a child of Path
  • Rename it to "PathFollow2"
  • Drag your second enemy onto PathFollow2 in the Scene dock
  • In the Inspector, set its Position to (0, 0, 0)
  • Open your second enemy scene and check its move_speed is different from your basic enemy. Change it if it is the same.
  • Press F6. Both enemies walk, and the faster one pulls ahead.
💡 GDD Check: Check your GDD. Do your two enemy types feel different enough to matter?
